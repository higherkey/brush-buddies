# Tobee and the Runaway Ball of Yarn

**Buddy:** Toebean the Cat (Tobee)

**Destination:** Tobee's Yarn Cottage

**Stops:** Yarn Basket → Sunny Windowsill → Cushion Nook  
**Goal item:** Ball of Yarn  
**Read-aloud time:** About 5 minutes

Toebean the Cat, or Tobee to her friends, had a very important job to do.

Today was the day she would make the softest, warmest blanket in the whole cottage. It would be just the right size for a little mouse who had been shivering outside her window every evening.

Tobee had already chosen her favorite yarn: a ball of blue wool as round as a blueberry and as fluffy as a cloud.

There was only one problem.

The ball of yarn was missing.

“Meow?” Tobee asked the empty room.

She checked under her paws. She checked behind her ears. She even checked inside the teapot, although she was quite sure she hadn't put it there.

“Perhaps,” she said, “I should begin where yarn belongs.”

Her first stop was the **Yarn Basket**.

The basket sat beside a rocking chair, full of red, yellow, and green yarn. Tobee nosed through it carefully. Red yarn. Yellow yarn. A little knot of green yarn that looked suspiciously like broccoli.

But no blue ball.

Then she noticed a single blue thread trailing over the basket's edge.

“A clue!” she cried.

Tobee followed the thread across the rug. It curled around the table leg, climbed a stack of books, and ended at a sunbeam.

Her second stop was the **Sunny Windowsill**.

The windowsill was Tobee's favorite place to watch the garden. Butterflies floated over the flowers, and the little mouse sat beneath a rosebush, rubbing his paws together.

“Oh dear,” Tobee whispered. “He really does need that blanket.”

Something blue fluttered behind the curtain.

Tobee pounced.

Pffft!

It was only a blue feather, carried inside by the breeze.

Tobee sat down, disappointed. She had wanted to find the yarn before the sun went down. Now the afternoon shadows were growing longer.

From outside came a tiny squeak.

“Are you looking for something?” asked the mouse.

“My blue yarn,” said Tobee. “I was going to make you a surprise. Except now I've spoiled the surprise and lost the yarn too.”

The mouse smiled. “You haven't spoiled anything. A kind surprise is still kind, even when someone knows about it.”

Tobee thought about that.

“Did you see where the yarn went?” she asked.

“I saw something round roll past the window,” said the mouse. “It bounced off the rocking chair.”

Tobee remembered the chair. It was right beside her third stop: the **Cushion Nook**.

The nook held three pillows, one patchwork quilt, and a cushion so enormous that Tobee sometimes disappeared into it completely.

She looked behind the first pillow. Nothing.

She lifted the quilt. Nothing.

Then the enormous cushion made a tiny noise.

“Brrrp.”

Tobee froze.

“Cushions,” she said slowly, “do not usually burp.”

She peeked behind it.

There sat the missing **Ball of Yarn**, tangled around the tail of a toy mouse. Every time the rocking chair moved, the toy had tugged the yarn farther into the nook.

Tobee laughed so hard she rolled onto her back.

“I've found you!”

She carefully unwound the thread, gathered the yarn, and settled into her rocking chair. Loop by loop, row by row, the little blanket grew. The mouse waited by the window while Tobee worked, telling her stories about the garden.

By evening, the blanket was finished.

Tobee carried it outside. The mouse wrapped it around his shoulders, and his whiskers lifted in a delighted smile.

“It's perfect,” he said.

Tobee looked back at her cozy cottage, where the yarn basket, sunny windowsill, and cushion nook glowed in the lamplight.

The best part of her adventure hadn't been finding the yarn.

It had been finding someone to share its warmth with.

And that night, two very contented friends watched the first stars appear.
