# Finn and the Little Acorn

**Buddy:** Finn the Fox  
**Destination:** Finn's Maple Den  
**Stops:** Maple Trail → Berry Hollow → Cozy Den  
**Goal item:** Little Acorn  
**Read-aloud time:** About 5 minutes

Finn the Fox liked to collect little things that told big stories.

He had a feather from a windy day, a pebble from the river, and a leaf shaped almost exactly like a heart.

But there was one thing missing from his collection: a little acorn from the oldest oak in the woods.

Grandmother Fox had told him that the oak grew beside the **Maple Trail**, and that its acorns were small and round, with caps like tiny knitted hats.

Finn packed a satchel, tied his red scarf, and set out.

The trail was covered with orange and golden leaves. Every step made a pleasant crunch.

Crunch, crunch, crunch.

At the first bend, Finn found an acorn.

“Already?” he said.

He held it up to the light. It was shiny and beautiful, but much too big.

A squirrel hurried past carrying three nuts.

“Excuse me,” Finn called. “Do you know the oldest oak?”

“Of course,” said the squirrel. “But the path is hard to follow today. The wind has buried the signposts.”

Finn looked at the fallen leaves. The trail did seem to vanish beneath them.

Together they cleared the first sign, then the next. The squirrel thanked Finn and pointed toward **Berry Hollow**.

“Follow the sweet smell of berries. You'll find the oak beyond the hollow.”

Berry Hollow was busy with birds picking the last fruit of autumn. Finn's stomach rumbled.

He picked one berry, then stopped when he heard a soft sniffle.

A young hedgehog sat beside an empty basket.

“I promised my family berry muffins,” she said, “but the best berries are too high for me to reach.”

Finn looked at the branches. He could jump, but the hedgehog could not.

“Let's work together,” he said.

Finn gently shook a branch while the hedgehog held the basket underneath. Plip, plop, plip! Berries fell like little red raindrops.

Soon her basket was full.

“For helping me,” said the hedgehog, “you may have this.”

She offered him a perfectly round acorn.

Finn turned it over in his paws.

It had a tiny knitted-hat cap.

“Where did you find it?” he whispered.

“Under the old oak, yesterday.”

Finn thanked her and tucked the **Little Acorn** into his satchel.

He had found what he came for, but the sky was turning lavender. He needed to get home.

At the edge of Berry Hollow, he realized he could no longer see the trail.

For a moment his heart beat fast.

Then he remembered the signs he and the squirrel had uncovered. He followed them carefully, one by one, until the familiar maple trees appeared.

By the time he reached his **Cozy Den**, the first evening stars were shining.

Grandmother Fox was waiting with a lantern and a warm bowl of soup.

“Did you find your treasure?” she asked.

Finn placed the little acorn on the table.

“I did,” he said. “But I also found a squirrel who needed help with signs, and a hedgehog who needed help with berries.”

Grandmother smiled. “Then you brought home more than an acorn.”

Finn considered this as he ate his soup.

Afterward, he put the acorn on a shelf beside his feather, pebble, and heart-shaped leaf. He wrote a tiny label beneath it:

*The day I learned that helping others can help you find your way.*

And every time Finn looked at that acorn, he remembered not only the old oak, but the friends he had met along the way.
